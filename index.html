CASE 01 · PRODUCTION SYSTEM · n8n + AI

VIS Global — end-to-end outbound system

n8n · NocoDB · xAI Grok · GPT-4 · Apollo · Apify · Unipile · Live in production
System Architecture
Problem

VIS Global — a 20+ year enterprise tech firm with clients including Viacom and Cox — wanted to expand its U.S. and EU pipeline across four hard-to-bundle product lines (Contact Center & CX, AV & Collaboration, Network & Security, and proprietary automation products like DiGON and RADIUS). The challenge: every prospect needs a different message depending on industry and product fit, but the team can't write 100+ unique emails a week by hand.

Approach

Built six interconnected n8n workflows — three for the email cadence, one for sequence cleanup, and two for parallel LinkedIn outreach. Each runs daily on cron triggers; the LinkedIn DM responder fires on webhook.

  • Lead sourcing layered: Apollo for verified emails, Apify for LinkedIn profile signal, SerpAPI for recent news + intent triggers.
  • Two-table NocoDB schema (Leads + Emails) with relational linking via the link-records API — every email back-references its lead, every lead carries its full email history.
  • AI personalization at send time: each prompt carries the full VIS product portfolio plus the specific lead's company, role, and recent activity. Grok writes the cold email; GPT-4 handles the LinkedIn voice.
  • State separation: sequence_status for email flow, connection_status for LinkedIn — so a lead can be mid-email AND mid-connection without state collisions.
Stack

Result

A live production system running daily at VIS Global. Every lead is measurable from first touch through reply, bounce, connection accept, and DM exchange. Manual outreach work became a self-running, AI-personalized program handling the entire top of funnel for U.S. operations.

Email flow — 11 tracked states
empty → email1_sent → email2_sent → email3_sent → completed
BRANCHES · replied bounced soft_bounce blocked unsubscribed error
LinkedIn flow — 7 tracked states
not_sent → pending → accepted → dm_sent → dm_replied

Because every state transition is logged, campaign performance is decomposable: which emails open, which subjects bounce, which roles warm faster on LinkedIn vs. email. Each iteration sharpens the next.

What I'd Do Differently

Replace Apollo lead sourcing with a custom scraper on LinkedIn Sales Navigator + Clearbit — Apollo's data freshness was inconsistent and credit costs added up. Move AI personalization from per-lead inline calls to a batch generation queue running off-peak — would cut OpenRouter token costs by ~60% and let me A/B test prompt variants in parallel. Finally, layer a campaign-level dashboard in Power BI or Tableau over NocoDB — right now sequence health is visible per-lead but hard to read at the campaign level. Open question I'm still working on: what's the right "send rate vs. deliverability" curve at scale.

Problem

A regional coffee chain wanted to know which products and dayparts drove repeat purchases — but the POS exports were four disconnected CSVs with no shared schema, no time-of-day rollups, and no segments. Leadership was making merchandising calls on gut feel.

Approach

Normalized the four CSVs into a star schema. Added a date dimension with morning · midday · afternoon · evening buckets. Used window functions (LAG, RANK, NTILE) for cohort and product-pair analysis, then layered an RFM segmentation to find loyalty tiers.

Problem

NPS scores were declining but the post-flight survey data was unstructured — thousands of free-text comments. Ops didn't know where to focus: cabin, delays, food, baggage, or seating. Response counts varied wildly across routes, making raw averages misleading.

Approach

Built a 4-stage pipeline (above): clean text → NLTK classify into 6 categories → normalize by route volume so small routes weren't drowned out → render in a Tableau dashboard with LOD expressions that recompute correctly across user filters.

Problem

Attrition climbing, but HR couldn't say why with specificity. Exit ratings, tenure, satisfaction, and manager-change events sat in three different HRIS systems — no unified view, no early-warning signal.

Approach

Built a unified employee fact table from disparate HRIS exports. Defined 6 attrition signals (above) and ranked them by predictive weight. A 3-tab Power BI report: attrition overview · department heatmap · watch-list view filtering on multi-signal risk. DAX time intelligence for rolling 6-month rate; star schema for filter performance.
